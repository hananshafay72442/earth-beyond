# Parallel Solar System
## Space Exploration, Colony Building & Tower Defense

> **Working title:** Parallel Solar System  
> **Genre:** Sci-fi strategy / exploration / colony building / tower defense  
> **Platform:** Web browser  
> **Suggested stack:** Flask + Python + JavaScript + HTML/CSS + MySQL

---

# Name Ideas

The game needs a name that immediately communicates its premise rather than sounding like a generic sci-fi title.

### Strong candidates

1. **Parallel Solar System** — clearest and most self-explanatory.
2. **The Other Solar System** — simple, mysterious, and easy to remember.
3. **Parallel Earth** — emphasizes the alternate-Earth mystery.
4. **Earth: Another World** — more dramatic, but slightly less precise.
5. **Second Solar System** — short and intriguing.
6. **The Other Earth** — strong story focus.
7. **Parallel Frontier** — good branding, but less self-explanatory.
8. **Solar System: Elsewhere** — atmospheric and distinctive.
9. **Earth Beyond** — more commercial-sounding, but less descriptive.
10. **The Unfamiliar Solar System** — very clear, but probably too long for a final title.

### Recommended name

**Parallel Solar System**

It immediately tells a new player what the game's central idea is: this is a familiar Solar System, but not ours.

# 1. Game Concept

**Parallel Solar System** is a browser-based strategy game combining:

- Space exploration
- Colony/base building
- Resource management
- Research
- Tower defense
- Planetary progression
- Story and mystery

The player commands a team of human scientists aboard an experimental spacecraft.

During an experiment involving spacetime, the ship is transported into an alternate universe.

They discover something extraordinary:

> **This universe has a Solar System remarkably similar to ours, but humanity never evolved here.**

Instead, other forms of life evolved.

Some worlds contain intelligent species.

One world in particular contains an extremely advanced civilization.

That world is the alternate version of **Earth**.

And it is the hardest and most advanced world in the game.

# 2. Opening Story

The game should begin with a short story sequence before gameplay.

## The Asteria

Year 2189.

Humanity has developed advanced spacecraft, but interstellar travel is still extremely difficult.

The research vessel **Asteria** is conducting an experiment designed to study an artificial gravitational anomaly.

Something goes wrong.

The ship's instruments temporarily record impossible readings.

The stars disappear.

Then they return.

A familiar star system appears ahead.

At first, the crew believes they have somehow returned to the Solar System.

The planets appear familiar:

**Mercury. Venus. Earth. Mars. Jupiter. Saturn. Uranus. Neptune.**

But the measurements are wrong.

The star is slightly different.

The orbital parameters don't match.

The radio environment is unfamiliar.

Then the crew observes Earth.

The planet has artificial lights across its night side.

It is inhabited.

A scan reveals:

- Liquid water
- Oxygen
- A complex atmosphere
- Life
- Artificial structures

Then the ship receives a transmission.

It is not human.

It is intelligent.

The translated message says:

> **"Unknown vessel. You have entered restricted orbital territory."**

The crew realizes the truth.

They did not return home.

They entered another universe.

And someone already knows they are there.

# 3. The Core Gameplay Loop

The game should revolve around five activities:

```text
EXPLORE
   ↓
DISCOVER
   ↓
COLLECT
   ↓
BUILD & RESEARCH
   ↓
DEFEND
   ↓
UPGRADE
   ↓
EXPLORE FURTHER
```

The player explores planets to find resources, anomalies, ruins, native settlements, and technology.

Resources allow the player to build and upgrade a colony.

Expanding activity attracts attention from native species, eventually triggering attacks.

Successful defense gives the player resources, research opportunities, and access to more dangerous areas.

- Rocky
- Very hot
- Small
- Resource-rich
- Dangerous surface conditions
- Small colony capacity

## Venus-like World

- Extremely dense atmosphere
- Extreme greenhouse effect
- Very high surface temperature
- Valuable atmospheric resources
- Requires advanced protective equipment

## Earth-like World

- Liquid water
- Oxygen-rich atmosphere

- Advanced technology

## Mars-like World

- Cold
- Thin atmosphere
- Large exploration areas
- Ancient geological structures
- Valuable minerals

## Jupiter-like World

- Moons
- Atmospheric resources
- Radiation hazards
- Orbital stations
- Resource extraction

## Saturn-like World

- Gas giant
- Large ring system
- Many moons
- Mining opportunities
- Difficult navigation

## Uranus-like World

- Ice giant
- Extremely cold
- Unusual axial orientation
- Remote moons
- Rare resources

## Neptune-like World

- Extremely distant
- Very cold
- Powerful atmospheric activity
- Extremely difficult to reach
- Late-game exploration

# 5. Earth Is the Endgame

The Earth-like planet should be the most difficult major world.

It is not simply the final level because it has stronger enemies.

It is difficult because the native civilization has had thousands of years to develop on its own planet.

They have:

- Cities
- Industrial infrastructure
- Advanced research
- Spaceflight
- Military technology
- Orbital defenses
- Deep knowledge of their planet
- A huge home-field advantage

The player should initially be unable to compete directly.

Instead, they must:

1. Scout
2. Research
3. Establish colonies elsewhere
4. Develop advanced technology
5. Reverse-engineer alien technology
6. Build a stronger fleet
7. Gather intelligence
8. Eventually confront the Earth civilization

The Earth-like world should feel like entering the territory of a civilization that may be more advanced than humanity.

- Mine
- Storage
- Reactor
- Research Laboratory
- Radar

# 8. Resources

Keep the initial economy relatively small.

### Iron
Basic construction.

### Titanium
Advanced structures and weapons.

### Energy Crystals
Power infrastructure.

### Biomass
Biological research.

### Rare Elements
High-level technology.

### Alien Technology
Obtained by studying native artifacts and facilities.

The economy should exist to support exploration, research, construction, and defense rather than becoming a spreadsheet simulator.

---

# 9. Power System

Every major colony structure consumes energy.

Example:

```text
Energy production: 100

Command Center       -10
Mining Facility      -20
Research Lab         -15
Radar                -10
Laser Tower          -20
Shield Generator     -30
```

During an attack, the player may need to make decisions such as:

> Disable mining to power the shield.

or:

> Reduce shield power to activate another weapon.

This makes the colony an interconnected system rather than a collection of independent buildings.

---

# 10. Tower Defense

Native attacks should be connected to player activity.

For example:

1. The player discovers a valuable mineral.
2. They begin extracting it.
3. Local activity increases.
4. Native scouts detect the colony.
5. The first attack occurs.
6. Larger attacks follow as the player expands.

This makes attacks feel like consequences of the player's actions rather than random waves.

## Defense Types

### Machine Gun Tower
Cheap and effective against weak ground units.

### Laser Tower
High accuracy and strong sustained damage.

### Missile Battery
Long-range explosive damage.

### EMP Tower
Disables mechanical enemies.

### Shield Generator
Protects nearby buildings.

### Railgun
Extremely powerful but slow and expensive.

---

# 11. Enemy Types

Enemies should have distinct purposes.

### Crawlers
Fast, weak units that overwhelm defenses.

### Armored Units
Slow, durable units designed to break defensive lines.

### Burrowers
Travel underground and bypass normal walls.

### Flyers
Attack from above and ignore ground paths.

### Saboteurs
Target reactors, research labs, and other important structures.

### Shield Units
Protect nearby enemies.

### Siege Units
Attack defenses from long range.

### Elite Units
Rare high-level enemies with unique abilities.

The goal is to force players to adapt their defenses rather than finding one perfect layout.

# 12. Native Civilizations

The native species should not be generic monsters.

They evolved independently in this universe.

They have:

- Their own biology
- History
- Technology
- Culture
- Political structures
- Motivations

Some species may be:

- Hostile
- Neutral
- Trade-oriented
- Territorial
- Curious about humans
- Interested in human technology

The player should gradually learn that humans are the outsiders.

# 13. Alien Technology

Native technology provides an alternative progression path.

```text
Human Technology
       ↓
Alien Artifact
       ↓
Scientific Analysis
       ↓
Reverse Engineering
       ↓
Hybrid Technology
```

Alien technology should be powerful but difficult to understand.

Example:

The player discovers an alien energy system.

After research, it unlocks:

> Advanced Reactor

This produces dramatically more energy but requires rare resources.

---

# 14. Research

Research should connect discoveries to gameplay.

Instead of simply clicking an upgrade button, the player should investigate actual discoveries.

For example:

> **Unknown mineral discovered**

Scientists analyze it.

The game explains a real property or a fictional property inspired by real chemistry.

The result unlocks:

> High-Efficiency Heat Sink

This creates a connection between:

**Exploration → Science → Technology → Gameplay**

# 15. Space Travel

Travel between planets should be inspired by real orbital mechanics.

Missions can consider:

- Distance
- Fuel
- Cargo
- Travel time
- Risk
- Ship technology

Gameplay should simplify the real calculations so that travel remains enjoyable.

The science should influence decisions without becoming tedious.

# 17. The Central Mystery

The game should continuously raise one question:

> **Why does this universe resemble ours?**

Possible clues can include:

- Similar planetary arrangement
- Similar chemistry
- Similar biological structures
- Ancient ruins
- Unusual mathematical patterns
- Native records referring to another Earth
- Evidence that the anomaly was artificially created

The final explanation should be developed as the story matures.

Do not reveal the answer early.

# 18. Real Science in the Game

Scientific facts should influence gameplay wherever practical.

The game should clearly separate:

### Established science
Things strongly supported by observation and experiment.

### Science-inspired mechanics
Real concepts simplified for gameplay.

### Speculative science
Ideas that are scientifically discussed but not experimentally established.

### Fiction
Elements invented for the game's story.

Example:

```text
Planetary gravity          REAL
Orbital mechanics          REAL
Atmospheric pressure       REAL

Habitable zones            REAL
```

# 19. Scientific Concepts to Use

## Gravity

Surface gravity depends on mass and radius:

```text
g = G × M / r²
```

Gravity can affect:

- Movement
- Landing
- Projectile behavior
- Rocket requirements
- Exploration equipment

---

## Planetary Atmospheres

Atmospheric composition and pressure influence:

- Temperature
- Survival
- Weather
- Radiation exposure
- Landing requirements

Every planet should have an environmental profile.

---

## Venus and the Greenhouse Effect

Venus is hotter than Mercury despite being farther from the Sun.

Its dense atmosphere creates an extreme greenhouse effect.

A Venus-like planet should therefore require specialized equipment rather than merely having an arbitrary "hot" status.

---

## Radiation

Spacecraft and colonies can encounter:

- Solar radiation
- Cosmic radiation
- Radiation belts
- High-radiation planetary environments

Radiation can affect crew safety and electronics.

This gives the player a reason to research shielding.

---

## Habitable Zones

A star's habitable zone is the region where conditions may allow liquid water to exist on a planet's surface under suitable atmospheric conditions.

Being in the habitable zone does **not** guarantee life.

This distinction should be reflected in the game's scientific explanations.

---

## Planet Types

The real Solar System contains:

### Terrestrial planets
Mercury, Venus, Earth, Mars.

### Gas giants
Jupiter, Saturn.

### Ice giants
Uranus, Neptune.

The game's planetary counterparts should respect these broad physical differences.

---

## Jupiter

Jupiter is the largest planet in our Solar System and has a powerful magnetic environment.

A Jupiter-like world should therefore emphasize:

- Moons
- Radiation
- Orbital infrastructure
- Atmospheric research
- Resource extraction

rather than normal surface colonies.

---

## Saturn

Saturn's extensive ring system can inspire:

- Ring mining
- Ice resources
- Navigation hazards
- Hidden structures
- Moon colonies

---

## Uranus

Uranus is an ice giant with an unusual axial orientation.

This can inspire unusual seasonal and environmental mechanics.

---

## Neptune

Neptune is the most distant major planet in our Solar System and has powerful atmospheric activity.

Its game counterpart should feel remote, cold, and difficult to reach.

---

## Light-Speed Communication

Light travels at approximately:

**300,000 km/s**

Therefore, communication across large astronomical distances is delayed.

The game can display realistic communication delays for immersion without making them a major inconvenience.

---

## Solar System Formation

Our Solar System formed about **4.6 billion years ago** from a collapsing cloud of gas and dust.

This can inspire the game's planetary geology, asteroid belts, and resource distribution.

### One planet

Preferably the Mars-like planet.

### One colony

Include:

- Command Center
- Mine
- Storage
- Reactor
- Research Lab

### Four resources

- Iron
- Titanium
- Energy
- Biomass

### Four defenses

- Machine Gun Tower
- Laser Tower
- Missile Battery
- Shield Generator

### Four enemies

- Crawler
- Armored Unit
- Flyer
- Siege Unit

### Exploration

A small fog-of-war map with resource deposits and one or two points of interest.

### Research

Approximately 10 technologies.

### Story

One complete chapter ending with the first major attack.

The objective is simple:

> **Make one planet genuinely fun before expanding the game.**

# 26. Visual Direction

The game should look like:

**Scientific mission control + futuristic colony computer.**

Avoid directly copying Clash of Clans' cartoon presentation.

Use:

- Dark space backgrounds
- Planetary maps
- Glowing interfaces
- Scientific diagrams
- Radar displays
- Holographic panels
- Atmospheric effects
- Clean typography
- Subtle animations

The game should feel technologically advanced without requiring expensive 3D graphics.

---

# 27. Design Principles

### Exploration must matter
There should be a reason to investigate unknown areas.

### Building must create meaningful choices
Different layouts and power decisions should have consequences.

### Defense must adapt
There should not be one perfect base layout.

### Science must influence gameplay
Real concepts should be more than trivia.

### Aliens must have believable motivations
They are civilizations, not target dummies.

### Progression must feel earned
Players should unlock technology through exploration, research, and strategic decisions.

### Story must reward curiosity
Important discoveries should answer one question while creating another.

---

# 28. Final Vision

The finished game should make the player feel like humanity's first expedition into a parallel universe.

The player should look at an unexplored sector and think:

> **What is there?**

They should discover an ancient structure and think:

> **Who built this?**

They should see a native fleet approaching and think:

> **Can my colony survive?**

And when they finally reach the alternate Earth:

> **Why does this world look so much like ours?**

The game is ultimately about the discovery that humanity is not exploring an empty universe.

**Humanity is the alien species here.**
